\begin{abstract}
Is every word-hyperbolic group sofic? OpenAI recently constructed a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group $G$ that is not residually finite, with a Lean formalization. We show that $G$ is not sofic. Their rank argument against finite quotients uses a quotient only through its permutation matrices, and it survives approximation: a relation that fails on a small fraction of points costs a small rank error, and a word with few fixed points costs a small trace error. So approximate permutation models are excluded at a fixed positive accuracy, while soficity would supply them at every accuracy; moreover, one fixed nonidentity element of $G$ dies in every sofic quotient. We formalize the conclusion in Lean on top of OpenAI's development. Sandhu and Thom independently found a different non-sofic torsion-free hyperbolic group by the same kind of rank count. The same rank count, with group elements in place of permutation matrices, excludes Linnell division rings: if $K$ is a division ring of the characteristic $q$ fixed by the construction and $\Lambda$ is any group containing $G$, then $K[\Lambda]$ has no Linnell division ring. In such a division ring, the defining independence conditions inside certain free subgroups of $G$ would give the relevant matrices full rank, while OpenAI's finite-field interpolation identity forces their rank to collapse; no zero divisor is exhibited. OpenAI's torsion-free groups whose group algebras over $\F_2$ have zero divisors, or are not directly finite, already show that Linnell division rings need not exist in characteristic two; here the group is $G$ itself. We also show that $G$ is the free product of a free group and a torsion-free hyperbolic group that again has no Linnell division ring in characteristic $q$, and we prove barriers to extending the argument to hyperlinearity. The group and every input to the non-soficity argument are OpenAI's.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A group is \emph{sofic} if every finite piece of its multiplication table can be modelled by permutations of a nonempty finite set, with nonidentity elements moving almost every point \cite{Gromov1999,Weiss2000,ElekSzabo2005}. Residually finite groups and amenable groups are sofic. Non-sofic groups were first constructed in 2026 \cite{OpenAI26}, building on Kun's expander decomposition for property~(T) groups \cite{Kun2019} and the centralizer obstruction of Kun and Thom \cite{KunThom2019}; the argument of this note uses neither property~(T) nor expanders. Further examples followed \cite{FournierFacio26,KunThom26,SauerSchesler26}, among them OpenAI's finitely presented groups whose group algebras over a field of characteristic two are not directly finite \cite{OAIdf2,OAI197}, which are not sofic by a theorem of Elek and Szab\'o \cite[Corollary~4.7]{ElekSzabo2004}, as noted there. These works do not establish a non-sofic word-hyperbolic group. Chapman and Peled showed that a non-sofic hyperbolic group exists if random two-dimensional Linial--Meshulam complexes in a middle range of triangle densities typically have linear cocycle stability in permutations \cite{ChapmanPeled25}. The hyperbolic group admitting a nonsofic probability-measure-preserving action noted in \cite[Section~1]{KunThom26}, obtained from \cite{OpenAI26} and \cite[Corollary~E]{AlekseevThom25}, is a different assertion: nonsoficity of an action does not establish nonsoficity of its acting group. Whether every word-hyperbolic group is sofic remained open: Ozawa asked whether every hyperbolic group is hyperlinear \cite[Introduction and Section~7]{Ozawa2004}, and Pestov records the question for soficity as well \cite[Open question~9.2]{Pestov2008}. Arzhantseva conjectured that all hyperbolic groups are residually finite if and only if all hyperbolic groups are sofic \cite[Conjecture~2.8]{Arzhantseva2014}; see also \cite[Conjecture~6.6]{ArzhantsevaCherix2024}.

OpenAI then constructed a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group $G$ that is not residually finite \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI252}, with a Lean formalization of that theorem \cite{OAImath}. We show that the same group is not sofic. Independently, Sandhu and Thom constructed a different torsion-free word-hyperbolic group that is not sofic \cite{SandhuThom26}; see the end of this introduction.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The torsion-free word-hyperbolic group $G$ of \cite{OAI252} is not sofic.
\end{theorem}

The proof in \cite{OAI252} assumes a finite quotient $Q$ in which a finite set $\cR$ of \emph{rectangular words} survives. It then compares two ranks of matrices built from the regular representation of $Q$: a lower bound from traces, and an upper bound from the defining relations and a finite-field interpolation identity. The comparison contradicts a fixed numerical budget. That argument never uses subgroups, kernels, orders of elements or any other structure of $Q$. It uses only that the regular representation is a homomorphism into permutation matrices, and that the rectangular words act without fixed points. Both facts have approximate versions, and every step of the comparison degrades by an amount we control. Passing from sofic approximations to normalized ranks over a division ring goes back to Elek and Szab\'o, who used it to embed the group algebra of a sofic group in a regular ring with a rank function and to deduce stable finiteness \cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~4.7]{ElekSzabo2004}. Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} states the result with explicit constants.

Write $\mov(\alpha)$ for the fraction of points moved by a permutation $\alpha$ of a finite set, and $\fix(\alpha)=1-\mov(\alpha)$. The group $G$ has a finite presentation whose relators are \emph{triangle relators} $\theta_t$ together with two relators that the argument never uses (Section~\ref{sec:group}). The construction depends on a prime $q$ and an integer $r<q$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:quant}
Assign a permutation of a nonempty finite set $\Omega$ to every generator of $G$, and extend the assignment to words. If every rectangular word fixes at most a fraction $1/(r-1)^2$ of $\Omega$, then some triangle relator moves at least a fraction $\eps_0$ of $\Omega$. Here $\eps_0=1/(4r^2N(q-1))$ for any data satisfying the budget~\eqref{eq:budget}, where $N$ is the number of line labels, and $\eps_0=1/(6rq)$ for the data of \cite[Lemma~2.2]{OAI252}.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} follows because soficity supplies such assignments with both defects arbitrarily small (Lemma~\ref{lem:models}). The same argument applied to a sofic image of $G$ gives a stronger conclusion.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:residual}
Some fixed rectangular word $w_0\ne1$ of $G$ lies in the kernel of every homomorphism from $G$ to a sofic group. In particular every quotient of $G$ in which all rectangular words survive is non-sofic.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:residual} extends a consequence drawn in \cite[Corollary~1.2]{OAI252}: there, one fixed nonidentity element of $G$ is killed by every finite-dimensional linear representation over a commutative field. Finitely generated linear groups are residually finite \cite{Malcev1940} (see also \cite[Introduction and \S3]{Nica2013}), hence sofic \cite[Example~4.2]{Pestov2008}, so they are among the targets here. Since LEF, residually amenable and initially subamenable groups are sofic \cite[Example~4.5 and the paragraph before it]{Pestov2008}, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows that $G$ is none of these. For LEF this already follows from \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI252}, since $G$ is finitely presented and finitely presented LEF groups are residually finite \cite{VershikGordon1997}; and for finitely presented groups, initial subamenability is equivalent to residual amenability \cite[proof of Proposition~2.7]{Arzhantseva2014}.

\paragraph{Division rings.} The same matrices and the line certificate also obstruct division rings. Let $K$ be a division ring and $\Lambda$ a group. A \emph{Linnell division ring} for $K[\Lambda]$ is a division ring generated by a copy of $K[\Lambda]$ in which, for every subgroup $C\le\Lambda$, elements of $\Lambda$ in distinct right cosets of $C$ are left linearly independent over the division subring generated by $K[C]$ \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025}, \cite[Definition~2.2]{FSP2026}. Those sources state the definition for torsion-free $\Lambda$; we use the same condition for every group, and it fails for every group with torsion, since $K[\Lambda]$ then has zero divisors. The independence conditions go back to Hughes \cite{Hughes1970}, and the name to a division ring that Linnell constructed from the group von Neumann algebra \cite{Linnell1993}; see \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025} and \cite[Remark~2.3]{FSP2026}. Section~\ref{sec:linnell} recalls the definitions in full. Jaikin-Zapirain and Linton conjectured that for every field $K$ and every torsion-free group $\Lambda$, $K[\Lambda]$ has a Linnell division ring, unique up to $K[\Lambda]$-isomorphism \cite[Conjecture~1]{JZL2025}. Fisher and S\'anchez-Peralta state the same conjecture for crossed products over division rings \cite[Conjecture~1.2]{FSP2026}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:linnell}
Let $K$ be a division ring of characteristic $q$, and let $\Lambda$ be a group containing $G$. Then $K[\Lambda]$ has no Linnell division ring. Moreover, no division $K[\Lambda]$-ring is strongly Hughes-free in the sense of Linnell \cite{Linnell2006}, as recalled in \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025}, and no epic division $K[\Lambda]$-ring satisfies condition (HF) of \cite[Definition~2.2(1)]{FSP2026}.
\end{theorem}

Hughes-freeness itself is defined only for locally indicable groups, and we do not know whether $G$ is locally indicable; Theorem~\ref{thm:linnell} excludes the independence condition (HF) on which that notion rests. Since $G$ is torsion-free, and data as in Section~\ref{sec:group} exist for every sufficiently large prime $q$ \cite[Lemma~2.2]{OAI252}, the existence part of both conjectures fails in every sufficiently large prime characteristic $q$, each time for the group $G$ built from that prime.

These conjectures were already refuted in positive characteristic. A Linnell division ring contains $K[\Lambda]$, so $K[\Lambda]$ would have no zero divisors and would be directly finite. OpenAI constructed a finitely presented torsion-free group $\Lambda$ with nonzero $\alpha,\beta\in\F_2[\Lambda]$ such that $\alpha\beta=0$ \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI196}, and another for which $\F_2[\Lambda]$ is not directly finite \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI197}. Either one refutes the existence part of both conjectures over $\F_2$. For every odd prime $p$, Ivanov constructed finitely presented torsion-free groups whose group algebras over $\F_p$ have zero divisors \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Ivanov2026}, which refutes them over $\F_p$. In a companion note, building on \cite{OAI196,OAI197}, we show that Kaplansky's group-ring conjectures fail for torsion-free word-hyperbolic groups in every positive characteristic \cite{DMzd}. In particular there are such groups with zero divisors over every $\F_p$; for $p=2$ the group is that of \cite{OAI196}, for a suitable choice of its random data, and for odd $p$ the construction uses its machinery. For the same reason these groups have no Linnell division ring in characteristic $p$. They are different groups from $G$. Theorem~\ref{thm:linnell} concerns $G$ itself, which is not sofic by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and its proof counts ranks instead of exhibiting a zero divisor.

The proof (Section~\ref{sec:linnell}) uses the same matrices $Y_i=(x_{iuv})_{u,v\in[r]}$ as Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq} below, now with entries in $K[G]$. For each label $i$, the elements $x_{iuv}x_{i00}^{-1}$ with $(u,v)\ne(0,0)$ freely generate a subgroup $A_i$ of $G$, a \emph{line subgroup} (Lemma~\ref{lem:free}). In a Linnell or strongly Hughes-free division ring, left linear independence of the powers of one element of $A_i$ over the division closure of a normal subgroup with infinite cyclic quotient forces every $Y_i$ to have full rank $r$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:fullrank}), while the line certificate forces $\sum_i\rank Y_i<Nr/4$. Whether $\F_q[G]$ embeds in some division ring is open. If it does, $\F_q[G]$ embeds in a division ring but has no Linnell division ring. If it does not, $G$ is a further counterexample to \cite[Conjecture~1.1]{FSP2026}, which predicts such an embedding for every torsion-free group; that conjecture already fails for the groups of \cite{OAI196}, \cite{Ivanov2026} and \cite{DMzd}, whose group algebras have zero divisors.

Section~\ref{sec:structure} shows that $G$ is the free product of a free group and a finitely presented torsion-free hyperbolic group $G^\sharp$, and that $K[\Lambda]$ has no Linnell division ring whenever $\Lambda$ contains $G^\sharp$ and $K$ has characteristic $q$. Consequently neither $G^\sharp$ nor $G$ is bi-orderable. An Euler characteristic count shows that $G$ embeds in no fundamental group of a two-complex with nonpositive immersions in the sense of Wise \cite{Wise2003}. Section~\ref{sec:hyperlinear} makes precise what the argument says about hyperlinearity.

\paragraph{Independent work.} Sandhu and Thom \cite{SandhuThom26}, based on an AI-generated construction obtained by H.~Sandhu, give an explicit torsion-free word-hyperbolic group that is not sofic; their argument and ours were found independently. They take from \cite{OAI252} the marked lines, the triangle relations built from Cartesian products, the interpolation identity, and the comparison of real and finite-field ranks. Their real-rank bound is the one in Step~2 of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq}, from \cite[Theorem~6]{AHKPY2011}, and \cite[Lemma~2.4]{OAI252} passes it to $\F_q$ \cite[Lemmas~3.2 and~3.3]{SandhuThom26}. In place of the random incidence graph they use the explicit graph $D(5,q)$ of Lazebnik and Ustimenko, of girth at least ten, with one block at each point, and they give the complex a locally CAT($-1$) metric \cite[Theorem~A]{SandhuThom26}. Their approximate rank inequality for permutation assignments \cite[Theorem~3.1]{SandhuThom26} and their passage from soficity \cite[Section~5]{SandhuThom26} follow the same mechanism as Sections~\ref{sec:transfer} and~\ref{sec:sofic}. Through a theorem of Kapovich and Wise \cite{KapovichWise2000}, they also obtain a nontrivial torsion-free word-hyperbolic group with no nontrivial sofic quotient \cite[Corollary~B]{SandhuThom26}, which goes further than Corollary~\ref{cor:residual}.

\paragraph{What is ours.} The group, its construction, its hyperbolicity and torsion-freeness, the nontriviality of the rectangular words, the trace bound on the real rank, the block factorization, the interpolation certificate, the rank-reduction lemma and the exact rank comparison against finite quotients are all from \cite{OAI252}, and all are theorems of the Lean development \cite{OAImath}. We contribute the approximate version of the rank comparison (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}), the passage from soficity (Section~\ref{sec:sofic}), the explicit constants, and a Lean formalization of the headline statement; Sandhu and Thom found the approximate rank comparison and the passage from soficity independently, for their own group \cite[Theorem~3.1 and Section~5]{SandhuThom26}. We also contribute Theorem~\ref{thm:linnell} and the other results of Sections~\ref{sec:hyperlinear} and~\ref{sec:linnell}. The freeness of the line subgroups follows from \cite[Theorem~2.1 and Lemma~2.3]{OAI252} by the argument that gives the nontriviality of the rectangular words there. Appendix~\ref{app:sources} lists earlier sources for the inputs taken from \cite{OAI252}. Section~\ref{sec:verification} records exactly what was checked, and how.

\section{The group and the facts we use}\label{sec:group}

We recall the data of \cite[Section~2]{OAI252}. The Lean structure \lean{MarkedLineData} records the marked lines, the exceptional set, the degree and girth conditions and the budget; the two-block partitions and their coordinates are then chosen from these data (\lean{MarkedLineData.partition}).

\paragraph{Data.} A prime $q$, the integer $h=20$, and an integer $r$ with $r<q$. A subset $S\subset\F_q$ of size $\lfloor q/100\rfloor$. The point set $P=\F_q^h$ has $m=q^h$ points. A set $I$ of $N$ line labels: label $i$ has a mark $a_i\in S^h$, with distinct labels having distinct marks, and a nonzero direction; its punctured line is $\ell_i^\circ=\{a_i+tv_i:t\in\F_q^\times\}$, with $q-1$ points. For $z\in P$ put $I(z)=\{i:z\in\ell_i^\circ\}$. An exceptional set $E\subset P$. At each regular point $z\notin E$, the set $I(z)$ is split into two disjoint blocks, and each block $b$ carries a bijection $i\mapsto(\alpha_b(i),\beta_b(i))$ from $I_b$ onto $\Z/s_b\times\Z/t_b$ with $s_b,t_b\ge r$. Moreover $|I(z)|\ge4r^2$ for $z\notin E$, and the bipartite graph on $P\sqcup I$ with an edge $z\sim i$ whenever $z\in\ell_i^\circ$ has no cycle of length at most $12$ \cite[Lemma~2.2]{OAI252}; these two conditions are used only through \ref{F:rect}, \ref{F:geo} and \cite[Lemma~2.3]{OAI252}, and in Proposition~\ref{prop:euler}. The data satisfy the strict budget \cite[(2.2)]{OAI252}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 q>r,\qquad \frac{Nr}{4}>2m+r|E|.
\end{equation}
We write $\Gamma=Nr-4(2m+r|E|)$, a positive integer.

\paragraph{Presentation.} Let $[r]=\{0,\dots,r-1\}$. The generators are $x_{iuv}$ for $i\in I$ and $u,v\in[r]$; $L_{b,u,j}$ for $j\in\Z/s_b$; and $R_{b,v,k}$ for $k\in\Z/t_b$, over all blocks $b$. For every block $b$, label $i\in I_b$ and $u,v\in[r]$ there is a triangle relator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 \theta_{b,i,u,v}=L_{b,u,\alpha_b(i)+v}\;x_{iuv}\;R_{b,v,\beta_b(i)+u}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Two further relators kill the edges of a spanning tree of the three-vertex graph underlying the triangle complex of \cite{OAI252}; the argument below never uses them. The group $G$ is the group of this presentation, which is $\pi_1$ of that complex. We index triangle relators by $t=(b,i,u,v)$; their number is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T=r^2\sum_{z\notin E}|I(z)|\le r^2N(q-1).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Rectangular words.} For $i\in I$, $u\ne u'$ and $v\ne v'$ in $[r]$,
\[
 w_{i,u,u',v,v'}=x_{iuv}\,x_{iu'v}^{-1}\,x_{iu'v'}\,x_{iuv'}^{-1}.
\]
Let $\cR$ be the finite set of these words.

\paragraph{Facts from \cite{OAI252}.} Each is a theorem of the Lean development; Lean names are in the namespace \lean{OAI.Release075}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item\label{F:rect} Every rectangular word is nontrivial in $G$ \cite[(2.6)]{OAI252} (\lean{MarkedLineData.rectangular_nontriviality}).
\item\label{F:cert} \emph{Line certificate.} For arbitrary matrices $Y_i$ ($i\in I$) over $\F_q$, all of one size,
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\rank Y_i\le\sum_{z\in P}\rank\Bigl(\sum_{i\in I(z)}Y_i\Bigr).
\]
This is \cite[(2.13)--(2.14)]{OAI252}: product Lagrange interpolation on $S^h$ \cite[Lemma~1.3, Theorem~2.3]{Schauz2008} gives matrices $H(z)$ of rank at most one with $\sum_{z\in\ell_i^\circ}H(z)=-E_{ii}$, because entries restricted to a line have degree below $q-1$, and a polynomial of degree below $q-1$ sums to zero over $\F_q$ \cite[Chapter~I, \S2.1, Lemma]{Serre1973}, \cite[Theorem~3.3(i)]{Schauz2008}; hence $-\bigoplus_iY_i=\sum_zH(z)\otimes\sum_{i\in I(z)}Y_i$ (\lean{grid_line_certificate}, \lean{rank_budget_of_line_certificate}). No group is involved.
\item\label{F:red} \emph{Rank under reduction} \cite[Lemma~2.4]{OAI252}. If $M$ is an integer matrix whose rows have Euclidean norm at most $\sqrt r$ and $q>r$ is prime, then $\rank_{\F_q}M\ge\frac12\rank_\R M$ (\lean{rank_reduction_half}). The same bound follows from Soul\'e's bound on the torsion of a cokernel \cite[Lemma~1]{Soule1999}, part of an argument that Soul\'e credits to Gabber; see also \cite[Lemma~3.1]{Sauer2016}.
\item\label{F:geo} $G$ is torsion-free and word-hyperbolic \cite[Theorem~2.1, Lemma~2.3]{OAI252} (\lean{MarkedLineData.presentation_torsionFree}, \lean{MarkedLineData.presentation_wordHyperbolic}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Approximate permutation models}\label{sec:transfer}

For a permutation $\pi$ of a nonempty finite set $\Omega$ with $|\Omega|=D$, let $P(\pi)$ be its permutation matrix, $P(\pi)e_c=e_{\pi(c)}$. Then $P(\pi\rho)=P(\pi)P(\rho)$, $P(\pi)^{\mathsf T}=P(\pi^{-1})$, and $\tr P(\pi)=D\fix(\pi)$.

A \emph{model} assigns a permutation $\sigma(s)$ of $\Omega$ to each generator $s$. It extends to words by $\sigma(s^{-1})=\sigma(s)^{-1}$ and multiplication; this extension is a homomorphism from the free group. A model has \emph{relator defects} $\delta_t=\mov\sigma(\theta_t)$ and \emph{rectangle defect} $\eta=\max_{w\in\cR}\fix\sigma(w)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ineq}
Every model with $\eta\le1/(r-1)^2$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_t\delta_t\ \ge\ \frac{Nr}{4}-2m-r|E|\ =\ \frac{\Gamma}{4}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each label $i$ let $Y_i$ be the $rD\times rD$ matrix of $D\times D$ blocks $P(\sigma(x_{iuv}))$, $u,v\in[r]$. It has entries $0$ and $1$, and each row has exactly $r$ ones.

\emph{Step 1: traces.} Put $A_i=Y_iY_i^{\mathsf T}$. Then $\tr A_i=\sum_{u,v}\tr\bigl(P P^{\mathsf T}\bigr)=r^2D$, and
\[
 \tr A_i^2=\sum_{u,u',v,v'}\tr P\bigl(\sigma(x_{iuv}x_{iu'v}^{-1}x_{iu'v'}x_{iuv'}^{-1})\bigr).
\]
If $u=u'$ or $v=v'$ the word cancels freely, so the term is exactly $D$; there are $2r^3-r^2$ such index choices. The other $r^2(r-1)^2$ terms are $D\fix\sigma(w)$ for rectangular words $w$, each at most $\eta D$. So $\tr A_i^2\le(2r^3-r^2)D+r^2(r-1)^2\eta D$.

\emph{Step 2: real rank.} Since $A_i$ is positive semidefinite, Cauchy--Schwarz on its eigenvalues gives $\rank A_i\ge(\tr A_i)^2/\tr A_i^2$, so
\[
 \rank_\R Y_i\ \ge\ \frac{r^2D}{2r-1+(r-1)^2\eta}\ \ge\ \frac{rD}{2}\qquad\text{when }(r-1)^2\eta\le1.
\]
This is the bound of \cite[Theorem~6]{AHKPY2011} for the $0/1$ matrix $Y_i$, whose rows and columns each contain $r$ ones: four-cycles in its bipartite graph are counted by fixed points of rectangular words, their number $\square$ satisfying $4\square=D\sum\fix\sigma(w)$ over the $r^2(r-1)^2$ index choices with $u\ne u'$ and $v\ne v'$ in Step~1. The inequality $\rank A\ge(\tr A)^2/\tr A^2$ is classical; see \cite[Section~3]{AHKPY2011} and the proof of \cite[Lemma~2.2]{Alon2009}.

\emph{Step 3: rank over $\F_q$.} The rows of $Y_i$ have norm $\sqrt r$ and $q>r$, so \ref{F:red} gives $\rank_{\F_q}Y_i\ge rD/4$.

\emph{Step 4: local factorization with errors.} Fix a block $b$ at a regular point $z$ and a triangle $t=(b,i,u,v)$, and abbreviate $L=L_{b,u,\alpha_b(i)+v}$, $x=x_{iuv}$, $R=R_{b,v,\beta_b(i)+u}$. A point $p$ is fixed by $\sigma(\theta_t)=\sigma(L)\sigma(x)\sigma(R)^{-1}$ exactly when the column $c=\sigma(R)^{-1}(p)$ of $P(\sigma(x))$ agrees with that of $P(\sigma(L))^{-1}P(\sigma(R))$. Hence
\[
 P(\sigma(x))=P(\sigma(L))^{-1}P(\sigma(R))+\Delta_t,\qquad \rank\Delta_t\le\delta_tD,
\]
since $\Delta_t$ has at most $\delta_tD$ nonzero columns. For fixed $u,v$, as $i$ runs over $I_b$ the pair $(\alpha_b(i)+v,\beta_b(i)+u)$ runs over $\Z/s_b\times\Z/t_b$ exactly once. Therefore
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_b}Y_i=\Bigl(\sum_{j}P(\sigma(L_{b,u,j}))^{-1}\Bigr)_{u\in[r]}\Bigl(\sum_kP(\sigma(R_{b,v,k}))\Bigr)_{v\in[r]}+\Delta_b,
\]
a block column times a block row, with inner dimension $D$, plus a matrix $\Delta_b$ whose $(u,v)$ block is $\sum_{i\in I_b}\Delta_{(b,i,u,v)}$. This is the factorization \cite[(2.11)]{OAI252} with error $\Delta_b$, and $\rank\Delta_b\le D\sum_{t\in b}\delta_t$. Each regular point has two blocks, so $Z(z)=\sum_{i\in I(z)}Y_i$ satisfies
\[
 \rank Z(z)\le 2D+D\sum_{t\text{ at }z}\delta_t\quad(z\notin E),\qquad \rank Z(z)\le rD\quad(z\in E),
\]
where the second bound is the size of the matrix.

\emph{Step 5: certificate.} By \ref{F:cert} over $\F_q$, $\sum_i\rank Y_i\le\sum_z\rank Z(z)$. With Steps 3 and 4,
\[
 \frac{NrD}{4}\le 2(m-|E|)D+r|E|D+D\sum_t\delta_t.
\]
Divide by $D$.
\end{proof}

When every $\delta_t=0$ and $\eta=0$, which is the case of the regular representation of a finite quotient in which $\cR$ survives, Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq} contradicts~\eqref{eq:budget}; this is the argument of \cite[Section~2]{OAI252}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}]
Suppose $\eta\le1/(r-1)^2$ and every $\delta_t<\eps_0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq} gives $\Gamma/4\le\sum_t\delta_t<T\eps_0$.

For general data, $\Gamma\ge1$ and $T\le r^2N(q-1)$ by~\eqref{eq:T}, so $T\eps_0\le1/4$ when $\eps_0=1/(4r^2N(q-1))$, a contradiction.

For the data of \cite[Lemma~2.2]{OAI252}, fixed there for a sufficiently large prime $q$, its proof gives $N\ge cm/2$ and $r|E|\le m$, where $c=100^{-20}$ and $cr\ge100$. Then $3m\le6N/c\le0.06\,Nr$, so $\Gamma/4\ge Nr/4-3m\ge0.19\,Nr$. With $\eps_0=1/(6rq)$, $T\eps_0\le r^2N(q-1)/(6rq)<Nr/6<0.19\,Nr$, again a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The threshold is astronomically small, since $r\ge100^{21}$ and $q$ is a much larger prime, but it is fixed and positive, and it does not depend on $|\Omega|$.

\section{From soficity to models}\label{sec:sofic}

Let $d(\alpha,\beta)=\mov(\alpha^{-1}\beta)$ be the normalized Hamming distance on $\Sym(\Omega)$; it is bi-invariant. We use the standard definition: a group $H$ is sofic if for every finite $F\subset H$ and every $\eps>0$ there are a nonempty finite set $\Omega$ and a map $\varphi:H\to\Sym(\Omega)$ with
\[
 d\bigl(\varphi(gh),\varphi(g)\varphi(h)\bigr)\le\eps\quad(g,h\in F),\qquad d\bigl(\varphi(g),1\bigr)\ge1-\eps\quad(g\in F\setminus\{1\}).
\]
This is \cite[Definition~1.2]{ElekSzabo2005}, up to the normalization $\varphi(1)=1$ made there. It is equivalent to the forms with separation only bounded away from $0$: the proof of \cite[Theorem~1]{ElekSzabo2005} amplifies separation by tensor powers; see also \cite[Theorem~3.5 and its proof]{Pestov2008}. Equivalently, for a presentation $H=F/K$, soficity says that finitely many words in $K$ can be made to fix almost every point while finitely many words outside $K$ fix almost none, by permutations assigned to the generators; Lemma~\ref{lem:models} is the instance we need, with explicit constants.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:models}
Let $\pi:G\to H$ be a homomorphism to a sofic group such that $\pi(w)\ne1$ for every $w\in\cR$. Then for every $\eps>0$ there is a model with every $\delta_t\le5\eps$ and $\eta\le8\eps$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $F\subset H$ consist of $1$, the images of the generators and their inverses, and the images of all prefixes of the triangle relators and the rectangular words. Take $\varphi$ as in the definition for $F$ and $\eps$, and set $\sigma(s)=\varphi(\pi(s))$ for each generator $s$.

From $\varphi(1)\varphi(1)\approx_\eps\varphi(1)$ we get $d(\varphi(1),1)\le\eps$, and then $d(\varphi(\pi s^{-1}),\varphi(\pi s)^{-1})\le2\eps$. For a word $w=s_1^{e_1}\cdots s_k^{e_k}$ whose prefixes map into $F$, telescoping over prefixes and bi-invariance give
\[
 d\bigl(\varphi(\pi w),\sigma(w)\bigr)\le(k-1)\eps+2k_-\eps,
\]
where $k_-$ is the number of inverse letters. A triangle relator has $k=3$ and $k_-=1$, and $\pi(\theta_t)=1$, so $\delta_t=d(\sigma(\theta_t),1)\le4\eps+d(\varphi(1),1)\le5\eps$. A rectangular word has $k=4$ and $k_-=2$, and $d(\varphi(\pi w),1)\ge1-\eps$ because $\pi(w)\ne1$; so $\fix\sigma(w)\le\eps+7\eps=8\eps$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
If $G$ were sofic, Lemma~\ref{lem:models} with $H=G$, $\pi=\mathrm{id}$ and \ref{F:rect} would give models with every $\delta_t\le5\eps$ and $\eta\le8\eps$ for every $\eps>0$. Taking $\eps$ with $8\eps\le1/(r-1)^2$ and $5\eps<\eps_0$ contradicts Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:residual}]
Suppose each $w\in\cR$ survives under some homomorphism $\pi_w$ to a sofic group $H_w$. Finite direct products and subgroups of sofic groups are sofic \cite[Theorem~1(1)]{ElekSzabo2006}, so the image $H$ of $G$ in $\prod_{w\in\cR}H_w$ is sofic, and every $w\in\cR$ survives in $H$. Lemma~\ref{lem:models} and Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} give a contradiction. So some $w_0\in\cR$ dies under every homomorphism to a sofic group, and $w_0\ne1$ by \ref{F:rect}.
\end{proof}

\section{Beyond permutation models}\label{sec:remarks}

\subsection{Hyperlinearity}\label{sec:hyperlinear}

Countable sofic groups are hyperlinear \cite[Theorem~2]{ElekSzabo2005}, so if $G$ is hyperlinear, it separates soficity from hyperlinearity and answers \cite[Open question~3.4]{Pestov2008} in the negative. Non-hyperlinear groups were constructed recently \cite[Corollary~C]{ALT26}, \cite[Theorem~1.4]{SauerSchesler26}; see also the conditional construction of \cite{Thom26}. The present argument does not determine whether $G$ is hyperlinear, and it breaks in three places. The rank reduction \ref{F:red} needs integer matrices; the certificate \ref{F:cert} needs a finite field; and in unitary models a relator that holds approximately in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm need not hold off a low-rank error, so Step~4 fails. The three results below locate these breaks. With bounded denominators every step survives (Proposition~\ref{prop:height}). In the group von Neumann algebra of $G$ the trace and factorization steps hold exactly, while the inequality of the certificate is reversed (Proposition~\ref{prop:tracial}). And a hyperlinear quotient of $G$ that is not sofic exists as soon as unitary approximations keep every rectangular word away from the identity (Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion}). None of them decides whether $G$ is hyperlinear.

A \emph{unitary model} assigns a unitary matrix $U(s)$ of some size $d$ to every generator $s$, and extends to words as in Section~\ref{sec:transfer}. Write $\tau_d=\frac1d\tr$ and $\|A\|_2=\tau_d(A^*A)^{1/2}$ for the normalized trace and Hilbert--Schmidt norm. For a permutation $\pi$, $\tau_d(P(\pi))=\fix(\pi)$ and $\|P(\pi)-I\|_2^2=2\mov(\pi)$.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded denominators]\label{prop:height}
Let $\nu$ be a positive integer with $\nu^2r<q$, and let $U$ be a unitary model all of whose matrix entries lie in $\nu^{-1}\Z[i]$. If $\operatorname{Re}\tau_d(U(w))\le1/(r-1)^2$ for every rectangular word $w$, then
\[
 \frac{\nu^3}{2}\sum_t\|U(\theta_t)-I\|_2^2\ \ge\ \frac{Nr}{4}-2m-(r-2)|E|\ \ge\ \frac\Gamma4.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We follow the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq}, with $Y_i=(U(x_{iuv}))_{u,v\in[r]}$, a matrix of size $rd$.

\emph{Steps 1 and 2.} As there, $\tr(Y_iY_i^*)=r^2d$, and
\[
 \tr\bigl((Y_iY_i^*)^2\bigr)=\sum_{u,u',v,v'}\tr U\bigl(x_{iuv}x_{iu'v}^{-1}x_{iu'v'}x_{iuv'}^{-1}\bigr)
\]
is real. The $2r^3-r^2$ terms with $u=u'$ or $v=v'$ equal $d$, and the real parts of the other $r^2(r-1)^2$ terms are at most $d/(r-1)^2$. So $\tr\bigl((Y_iY_i^*)^2\bigr)\le2r^3d$ and $\rank_\C Y_i\ge(r^2d)^2/(2r^3d)=rd/2$.

\emph{Step 3.} Let $\rho(M)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\operatorname{Re}M&-\operatorname{Im}M\\ \operatorname{Im}M&\operatorname{Re}M\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ be the realification of a complex matrix; it is additive and multiplicative, and $\rank_\R\rho(M)=2\rank_\C M$. The integer matrix $\rho(\nu Y_i)$ has real rank at least $rd$, and each of its rows has norm $\nu\sqrt r$, since a row of $Y_i$ consists of $r$ rows of unitary matrices. By \ref{F:red} with $\nu^2r$ in place of $r$, its reduction $\widehat Y_i$ modulo $q$ has rank at least $rd/2$ over $\F_q$.

\emph{Step 4.} For a triangle $t=(b,i,u,v)$ with sides $L,x,R$ as in Step~4 of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq}, put $\Delta_t=U(x)-U(L)^*U(R)=U(L)^*\bigl(U(\theta_t)-I\bigr)U(R)$. Its entries lie in $\nu^{-2}\Z[i]$, and the factorization of Step~4 reads
\[
 \nu^2\sum_{i\in I_b}Y_i=\Bigl(\nu\sum_jU(L_{b,u,j})^*\Bigr)_{u\in[r]}\Bigl(\nu\sum_kU(R_{b,v,k})\Bigr)_{v\in[r]}+\nu^2\Delta_b
\]
over $\Z[i]$, where the $(u,v)$ block of $\Delta_b$ is $\sum_{i\in I_b}\Delta_{(b,i,u,v)}$. Apply $\rho$ and reduce modulo $q$. Since $\nu$ is invertible modulo $q$ and reduction does not increase the rank of an integer matrix, $\rank\sum_{i\in I_b}\widehat Y_i\le2d+2\sum_{t\in b}\rank\Delta_t$. A nonzero column of $\Delta_t$ is $a-a'$ with unit vectors $a\in(\nu^{-1}\Z[i])^d$ and $a'\in(\nu^{-2}\Z[i])^d$. Then $\operatorname{Re}\langle a,a'\rangle$ lies in $\nu^{-3}\Z$ and is less than $1$, so $\|a-a'\|^2=2-2\operatorname{Re}\langle a,a'\rangle\ge2\nu^{-3}$. Hence
\[
 \rank\Delta_t\le\frac{\nu^3}{2}\tr(\Delta_t^*\Delta_t)=\frac{\nu^3d}{2}\|U(\theta_t)-I\|_2^2.
\]

\emph{Step 5.} By \ref{F:cert} over $\F_q$, with $\widehat Z(z)=\sum_{i\in I(z)}\widehat Y_i$ of size $2rd$,
\[
 \frac{Nrd}{2}\le\sum_z\rank\widehat Z(z)\le4d(m-|E|)+2rd|E|+\nu^3d\sum_t\|U(\theta_t)-I\|_2^2.
\]
Divide by $2d$.
\end{proof}

For a permutation model, which has $\nu=1$, Proposition~\ref{prop:height} is Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq} in the form $\sum_t\delta_t\ge Nr/4-2m-(r-2)|E|$, which Step~5 of its proof also gives. Since $\Gamma\ge1$ and there are $T\le r^2N(q-1)$ triangle relators, some triangle relator has $\|U(\theta_t)-I\|_2^2\ge1/(2\nu^3r^2N(q-1))$, whatever the dimension. So unitary models with denominators bounded by a fixed $\nu<(q/r)^{1/2}$ cannot make every triangle defect small while every rectangular word has $\operatorname{Re}\tau_d\le1/(r-1)^2$. This is a barrier for bounded denominators only, and it says nothing about hyperlinearity: approximating arbitrary unitary matrices needs unbounded denominators.

A \emph{tracial von Neumann algebra} is a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state; it need not be finite-dimensional. Let $\lambda$ be the left regular representation of $G$ on $\ell^2(G)$, let $L(G)$ be the von Neumann algebra it generates, with trace $\tau(a)=\langle a\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle$, and put $\tau_r=\Tr_r\otimes\tau$ on $M_r(L(G))$. Assign $\lambda(s)$ to every generator $s$, and let $Y_i=(\lambda(x_{iuv}))_{u,v\in[r]}$ and $Z(z)=\sum_{i\in I(z)}Y_i$ in $M_r(L(G))$.

\begin{proposition}[The group von Neumann algebra]\label{prop:tracial}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Every defining relator of $G$ maps to $1$, and $\tau(\lambda(w))=0$ for every rectangular word $w$.
\item For $Y\in M_r(L(G))$ let $\rank_\tau Y=\tau_r(p)$, where $p$ is the projection onto the closure of the range of $Y$ in $\ell^2(G)^r$. Then $\rank_\tau Y_i\ge r^2/(2r-1)$ for every label $i$, while $\rank_\tau Z(z)\le2$ for $z\notin E$ and $\rank_\tau Z(z)\le r$ for $z\in E$. Hence
\[
 \sum_i\rank_\tau Y_i\ \ge\ \frac{Nr^2}{2r-1}\ >\ \frac{Nr}{4}\ >\ 2m+r|E|\ \ge\ \sum_z\rank_\tau Z(z),
\]
so $\rank_\tau$ reverses the inequality of the certificate \ref{F:cert}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) holds because $\lambda$ is a representation of $G$, and $w\ne1$ by \ref{F:rect}.

(b) Put $A=Y_iY_i^*$. Its range is dense in that of $Y_i$, so $p$ is also the range projection of $A$, and $pA=A$. Expanding as in Step~1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq} and using (a) gives $\tau_r(A)=r^2$ and $\tau_r(A^2)=2r^3-r^2$. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for the positive form $(a,b)\mapsto\tau_r(b^*a)$ gives $\tau_r(A)^2=\tau_r(pA)^2\le\tau_r(p)\,\tau_r(A^2)$, so $\rank_\tau Y_i\ge r^4/(2r^3-r^2)$. The factorization of Step~4 holds exactly in $M_r(L(G))$, so for $z\notin E$ we have $Z(z)=\Phi_z\Psi_z$ with $\Phi_z$ an $r\times2$ matrix over $L(G)$. The range projection of $Z(z)$ is at most that of $\Phi_z$, which has the same trace as the range projection of $\Phi_z^*$, by polar decomposition; the latter is a projection in $M_2(L(G))$, so its trace is at most $2$. For $z\in E$ the bound $\tau_r(1)=r$ is trivial. The chain of inequalities follows from~\eqref{eq:budget}.
\end{proof}

Part (a) makes $L(G)$ a countermodel: every statement about unitary tuples that holds in all tracial von Neumann algebras, whatever further elements, traces or projections it involves, holds in $L(G)$, where all defining relators hold exactly and every rectangular word has trace $0$. So no such statement implies that exact defining relators force $\max_w\operatorname{Re}\tau(w)>0$. In a matrix algebra they do: an assignment of unitary matrices satisfying all defining relators exactly is a representation of $G$, and some rectangular word lies in its kernel, because the proof of \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI252} puts a rectangular word in the finite residual of $G$, which every finite-dimensional linear representation kills \cite[Corollary~1.2]{OAI252}; Corollary~\ref{cor:residual} gives this again. A proof that $G$ is not hyperlinear must therefore use a property of matrix algebras, or of their ultraproducts, that fails in $L(G)$; Proposition~\ref{prop:height} uses one, bounded denominators. Part (b) locates the failure in the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq}: Steps 1, 2 and 4 hold exactly in $L(G)$, and the certificate is the step without a tracial analogue.

We use the characterization of hyperlinearity in \cite[Theorem~3.6]{Pestov2008}, which Pestov traces to R\u{a}dulescu \cite{Radulescu2000}: a group $H$ is hyperlinear if and only if for every finite $F\subset H$ and $\eps>0$ there is a map $\vartheta$ from $F$ to the unitary group of some size with $\|\vartheta(g)\vartheta(h)-\vartheta(gh)\|_2<\eps$ whenever $g,h,gh\in F$, $\|\vartheta(1)-I\|_2<\eps$ if $1\in F$, and $\|\vartheta(g)-\vartheta(h)\|_2\ge1/4$ for distinct $g,h\in F$.

\begin{proposition}[A criterion]\label{prop:criterion}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There are unitary models $U_n$, of sizes $d_n$, such that $\|U_n(\theta)-I\|_2\to0$ for every defining relator $\theta$ and $\liminf_n\|U_n(w)-I\|_2>0$ for every rectangular word $w$.
\item $G$ has a hyperlinear quotient in which every rectangular word survives.
\end{enumerate}
A quotient as in (ii) is a hyperlinear group that is not sofic, by Corollary~\ref{cor:residual}, and in (i) necessarily $d_n\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii). Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\N$, and for $g\in G$ choose a word $w_g$ representing it. Two words representing the same element differ by a product of conjugates of relators, so by unitary invariance of $\|\cdot\|_2$ their values under $U_n$ have distance tending to $0$. Hence $\tau_\omega(g)=\lim_\omega\tau_{d_n}(U_n(w_g))$ is well defined, and $\mathcal N=\{g:\lim_\omega\|U_n(w_g)-I\|_2=0\}$ is a normal subgroup of $G$. Let $\bar G=G/\mathcal N$; every rectangular word survives in $\bar G$. For $g\notin\mathcal N$ we have $\operatorname{Re}\tau_\omega(g)<1$ and $|\tau_\omega(g)|\le1$, so $|(1+\tau_\omega(g))/2|<1$.

Let $V_{n,k}(s)=(U_n(s)\oplus I_{d_n})^{\otimes k}$, a unitary model of size $e=(2d_n)^k$. For every word $w$, $\tau_e(V_{n,k}(w))=\bigl((1+\tau_{d_n}(U_n(w)))/2\bigr)^k$ and $\|V_{n,k}(w)-I\|_2\le k\|U_n(w)-I\|_2$. Given a finite $F\subset\bar G$ and $\eps>0$, choose a preimage $g$ of each $\bar g\in F$ and put $\vartheta(\bar g)=V_{n,k}(w_g)$. If $\bar g,\bar h,\bar g\bar h\in F$, then $\|\vartheta(\bar g)\vartheta(\bar h)-\vartheta(\bar g\bar h)\|_2\le k\|U_n(w_gw_hw_{gh}^{-1})-I\|_2$, whose $\omega$-limit is $0$, and similarly for $\vartheta(1)$. For distinct $\bar g,\bar h\in F$, $\|\vartheta(\bar g)-\vartheta(\bar h)\|_2^2=2-2\operatorname{Re}\tau_e(V_{n,k}(w_h^{-1}w_g))$, and since $h^{-1}g\notin\mathcal N$, the $\omega$-limit of $|\tau_e(V_{n,k}(w_h^{-1}w_g))|$ is $|(1+\tau_\omega(h^{-1}g))/2|^k$, which is below $1/2$ for large $k$. Choosing $k$ first and then $n$ in a set belonging to $\omega$ gives the maps required by \cite[Theorem~3.6]{Pestov2008}, so $\bar G$ is hyperlinear.

(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i). Let $\pi\colon G\to\bar G$ be such a quotient. Apply \cite[Theorem~3.6]{Pestov2008} with $\eps=1/n$ to the finite set consisting of $1$, the images of the generators and their inverses, and the images of all prefixes of the defining relators and of the rectangular words, and put $U_n(s)=\vartheta(\pi(s))$. The argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:models}, with $\|\cdot\|_2$ in place of the Hamming distance, bounds $\|U_n(w)-\vartheta(\pi(w))\|_2$ by a constant times $1/n$ for these words. So the relator defects tend to $0$, and every rectangular word stays at distance at least $1/4-O(1/n)$ from $I$.

Finally, if $d_n=d$ for infinitely many $n$ in (i), compactness of the unitary group gives a limit model that satisfies all defining relators exactly, that is, a representation of $G$ by $d\times d$ unitary matrices, in which no rectangular word maps to $I$. This contradicts \cite[Corollary~1.2]{OAI252}, since the proof of \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI252} puts a rectangular word in the finite residual of $G$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Linear soficity}

 In models by invertible matrices over $\F_q$ with the normalized rank metric, as in the linear sofic approximations of Arzhantseva and P\u{a}unescu \cite{ArzhantsevaPaunescu2017}, Steps 4 and 5 survive but Steps 1--3 do not: there are no $0/1$ entries and no real traces. So the argument says nothing about linear soficity, or about weak soficity in the sense of Glebsky and Rivera \cite{GlebskyRivera2008}.

\subsection{Where approximation is needed}

The argument needs the rectangle defect $\eta$ below $1/(r-1)^2$. A sofic approximation whose separation is only bounded below by a constant, such as the $1/4$ of \cite[Theorem~3.5]{Pestov2008}, would not suffice directly; it must first be amplified by tensor powers, as in the proof of \cite[Theorem~1]{ElekSzabo2005}, to the standard form with separation $1-\eps$ that Lemma~\ref{lem:models} uses.

\section{Division rings}\label{sec:linnell}

Throughout this section $K$ is a division ring of characteristic $q$. Its prime field $\F_q$ is central in $K$, and hence in every group ring $K[\Lambda]$. For a label $i$ put
\[
 Y_i=(x_{iuv})_{u,v\in[r]}\in M_r(K[G]);
\]
the matrices of Section~\ref{sec:transfer} are its images under permutation models.

Let $\phi\colon K[\Lambda]\to D$ be a ring homomorphism to a division ring. For a subgroup $C\le\Lambda$ let $D_C$ be the division closure of $\phi(K[C])$ in $D$, the smallest subring of $D$ that contains $\phi(K[C])$ and is closed under inverting its nonzero elements; it is a division ring. For subgroups $C\le C'$, $D$ is \emph{$(C,C')$-free} if $\sum_jd_j\phi(g_j)\ne0$ whenever $g_1,\dots,g_n\in C'$ lie in distinct right cosets of $C$ and $d_1,\dots,d_n\in D_C$ are nonzero; it is then $(C,C'')$-free for every $C''$ with $C\le C''\le C'$. Following \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025} and \cite[Definition~2.2]{FSP2026}, $D$ is \emph{epic} if $D_\Lambda=D$. An epic $D$ is \emph{Linnell} if it is $(C,\Lambda)$-free for every subgroup $C$, and \emph{strongly Hughes-free}, a notion due to Linnell \cite{Linnell2006}, if it is $(C,C')$-free whenever $C$ is normal in $C'$. Condition (HF) asks that whenever $C'\le\Lambda$ is finitely generated, $C$ is normal in $C'$ and $C'/C$ is infinite cyclic, generated by $tC$, the powers $\phi(t)^k$, $k\in\Z$, be left linearly independent over $D_C$. In \cite[Definition~2.2(1)]{FSP2026} a Hughes-free division ring is an injective epic one satisfying (HF), and the notion is defined only for locally indicable groups.

\subsection{Rank collapse}

A \emph{Sylvester matrix rank function} on a ring $R$ assigns to every matrix $M$ over $R$ a real number $\rk(M)\ge0$, with $\rk(M)=0$ for zero matrices, $\rk(1)=1$, $\rk(M_1M_2)\le\min\{\rk M_1,\rk M_2\}$, $\rk(M_1\oplus M_2)=\rk M_1+\rk M_2$, and $\rk\left(\begin{smallmatrix}M_1&M_3\\0&M_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\ge\rk M_1+\rk M_2$ \cite[Section~2.5]{FSP2026}. A ring homomorphism $\phi$ from $R$ to a division ring $D$ gives one, $\rk(M)=\rank_D\phi(M)$, and by a theorem of Malcolmson every integer-valued one arises in this way \cite{Malcolmson1980}; see \cite[Section~2.5]{FSP2026}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:collapse}
Every Sylvester matrix rank function on $K[G]$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\rk(Y_i)\ \le\ 2(m-|E|)+r|E|\ <\ \frac{Nr}{4}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The axioms give $\rk(M_1+M_2)\le\rk M_1+\rk M_2$, since $M_1+M_2=\begin{pmatrix}I&I\end{pmatrix}(M_1\oplus M_2)\begin{pmatrix}I\\I\end{pmatrix}$; $\rk(M)\le k$ for a matrix $M$ with $k$ columns, since $M=MI_k$ and $\rk(I_k)=k$; and $\rk(-M)=\rk(M)$.

\emph{Local factorization.} The triangle relators give $x_{iuv}=L_{b,u,\alpha_b(i)+v}^{-1}R_{b,v,\beta_b(i)+u}$ in $G$. As in Step~4 of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq}, for each block $b$ and fixed $u,v$, the pair $(\alpha_b(i)+v,\beta_b(i)+u)$ runs over $\Z/s_b\times\Z/t_b$ exactly once as $i$ runs over $I_b$, so
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_b}Y_i=\Bigl(\sum_jL_{b,u,j}^{-1}\Bigr)_{u\in[r]}\Bigl(\sum_kR_{b,v,k}\Bigr)_{v\in[r]},
\]
a column times a row. At a regular point $z$ the two blocks give $Z(z)=\sum_{i\in I(z)}Y_i=\Phi_z\Psi_z$ with $\Phi_z$ of size $r\times2$, so $\rk Z(z)\le2$. For $z\in E$, $\rk Z(z)\le r$.

\emph{Certificate.} In \ref{F:cert}, $H(z)=f(z)f(z)^{\mathsf T}$ for a column $f(z)\in\F_q^N$ \cite[Section~2.5]{OAI252}, and the identity $-\bigoplus_iY_i=\sum_zH(z)\otimes Z(z)$ uses only that the Kronecker product is $\F_q$-bilinear; so it holds over $K[G]$. Since $H(z)\otimes Z(z)=(f(z)\otimes I_r)\,Z(z)\,(f(z)^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_r)$,
\[
 \sum_i\rk(Y_i)=\rk\Bigl(-\bigoplus_iY_i\Bigr)\le\sum_{z\in P}\rk Z(z)\le2(m-|E|)+r|E|,
\]
which is less than $Nr/4$ by~\eqref{eq:budget}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Freeness and full rank}

Fix a label $i$, and put $y_{uv}=x_{iuv}x_{i00}^{-1}$ and $A_i=\langle y_{uv}:u,v\in[r]\rangle\le G$; note that $y_{00}=1$. We call $A_i$ a \emph{line subgroup}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:free}
$A_i$ is free on $\{y_{uv}:(u,v)\ne(0,0)\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be the triangle complex of \cite[Section~2.3]{OAI252}, with vertices $O,V,W$, edges $L_{b,u,j}\colon O\to V$, $x_{iuv}\colon V\to W$ and $R_{b,v,k}\colon O\to W$, and one triangle for each triangle relator. The presentation of Section~\ref{sec:group} identifies $G$ with $\pi_1(X,O)$: if $\gamma_V,\gamma_W$ are the paths from $O$ in the spanning tree, the generator $x_{iuv}$ is the class of $\gamma_Vx_{iuv}\bar\gamma_W$. So $y_{uv}$ is the class of $\gamma_V\,x_{iuv}\bar x_{i00}\,\bar\gamma_V$. Let $\Theta_i\subset X$ be the graph with vertices $V,W$ and the $r^2$ edges $x_{iuv}$. Its fundamental group $\pi_1(\Theta_i,V)$ is free on the loops $x_{iuv}\bar x_{i00}$ with $(u,v)\ne(0,0)$, so it suffices that $\pi_1(\Theta_i,V)\to\pi_1(X,V)$ is injective.

A nontrivial element of $\pi_1(\Theta_i,V)$ is freely homotopic in $\Theta_i$ to a nonempty cyclically reduced closed edge path. Each of its turns is at $V$ or $W$ and compares the directions of two distinct edges $x_{iuv}$ and $x_{iu'v'}$ with the same label $i$. By \cite[Lemma~2.3]{OAI252} their link distance is at least $14\pi/5$ or infinite, so by the closed-path assertion of \cite[Theorem~2.1]{OAI252}, proved as \cite[Proposition~3.3]{OAI252}, the path is not nullhomotopic in $X$. This is the argument that gives \ref{F:rect} in \cite[(2.6)]{OAI252}, applied to all closed paths in $\Theta_i$.
\end{proof}

Let $\delta_i\colon A_i\to\Z$ be the homomorphism with $\delta_i(y_{uv})=-1$ for $u\ne v$ and $\delta_i(y_{uu})=0$, let $A_i^0=\ker\delta_i$, and let $t_i=y_{01}^{-1}$, so that $\delta_i(t_i)=1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fullrank}
Let $\Lambda$ be a group containing $G$, and $\phi\colon K[\Lambda]\to D$ a ring homomorphism to a division ring such that the powers $\phi(t_i)^k$, $k\in\Z$, are left linearly independent over $D_{A_i^0}$. Then $\phi(Y_i)$ is invertible in $M_r(D)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\hat t=\phi(t_i)$ and $D_0=D_{A_i^0}$. Conjugation by $\hat t$ is an automorphism of $D$ that maps $\phi(K[A_i^0])$ onto itself, because $A_i^0$ is normal in $A_i$; hence it maps $D_0$ onto itself. Call its restriction $\gamma$. By hypothesis the sum $R=\sum_{k\in\Z}D_0\hat t^k$ is direct, and $\hat td=\gamma(d)\hat t$ for $d\in D_0$, so $R$ is a subring of $D$, a skew Laurent polynomial ring over $D_0$. For $f=\sum_ka_k\hat t^k\ne0$ let $\deg f$ be the largest $k$ with $a_k\ne0$. The coefficient of $\hat t^{\deg f+\deg g}$ in $fg$ is $a_{\deg f}\gamma^{\deg f}(b_{\deg g})\ne0$, so $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$.

The ring $R$ satisfies the left Ore condition: nonzero $a,b\in R$ have nonzero $f,g\in R$ with $fa=gb$. Indeed, multiplying $a$ and $b$ on the left by powers of the unit $\hat t$, we may assume that their lowest exponents are $0$; let their degrees be $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and let $R_n=\bigoplus_{k=0}^nD_0\hat t^k$, a left $D_0$-space of dimension $n+1$. For $n\ge\alpha+\beta$ the left $D_0$-linear maps $f\mapsto fa$ on $R_{n-\alpha}$ and $g\mapsto gb$ on $R_{n-\beta}$ are injective with values in $R_n$, and $(n-\alpha+1)+(n-\beta+1)>n+1$, so their images meet nontrivially. Consequently the elements $s^{-1}f$ of $D$ with $s,f\in R$ and $s\ne0$ form a division subring $D'$, in which finitely many elements have a common left denominator. (For products, $fs^{-1}=s_1^{-1}f_1$ whenever $s_1f=f_1s$ with $s_1\ne0$; for sums, use a common left multiple of the denominators.)

Put $Y'=(y_{uv})_{u,v\in[r]}=Y_i\,x_{i00}^{-1}$. Each entry is $y_{uv}=n_{uv}t_i^{\delta_i(y_{uv})}$ with $n_{uv}\in A_i^0$, so $\phi(y_{uv})=\phi(n_{uv})\hat t^{\delta_i(y_{uv})}$ lies in $R$, with a unit of $D_0$ as its only coefficient; its degree is $0$ if $u=v$ and $-1$ otherwise. Suppose that $c\,\phi(Y')=0$ for a nonzero row $c$ over $D'$. Clearing a common left denominator, we may take $c$ over $R$. Let $M$ be the largest degree of a nonzero entry of $c$, attained at $c_{u^*}$, and consider entry $u^*$ of $c\,\phi(Y')$, namely $\sum_uc_u\phi(y_{uu^*})$. The term $u=u^*$ has degree $M$, and every other nonzero term has degree at most $M-1$. So this entry is nonzero, a contradiction. The rows of $\phi(Y')$ are therefore left linearly independent over the division ring $D'$, so $\phi(Y')$ is invertible over $D'$, hence over $D$, and so is $\phi(Y_i)=\phi(Y')\phi(x_{i00})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linnell}]
Let $\phi\colon K[\Lambda]\to D$ be a ring homomorphism to a division ring. If the powers of $\phi(t_i)$ were left linearly independent over $D_{A_i^0}$ for every $i$, Lemma~\ref{lem:fullrank} would make every $\phi(Y_i)$ invertible, so that $\sum_i\rank_D\phi(Y_i)=Nr$. Restricting $\phi$ to $K[G]$ and applying Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} gives $\sum_i\rank_D\phi(Y_i)<Nr/4$. So for some $i$ the powers of $\phi(t_i)$ are left linearly dependent over $D_{A_i^0}$.

The elements $t_i^k$ lie in distinct right cosets of $A_i^0$. So this dependence contradicts $(A_i^0,\Lambda)$-freeness, which every Linnell division ring has; $(A_i^0,A_i)$-freeness, which every strongly Hughes-free division ring has, as $A_i^0$ is normal in $A_i$; and condition (HF) for the finitely generated subgroup $A_i$, as $A_i/A_i^0$ is infinite cyclic, generated by $t_iA_i^0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The free factor and further consequences}\label{sec:structure}

Let $X^\sharp$ be obtained from $X$ by splitting $O$ into one vertex $O_b$ for each block $b$, $V$ into $V_u$ and $W$ into $W_v$ for $u,v\in[r]$, with edges $L_{b,u,j}\colon O_b\to V_u$, $x_{iuv}\colon V_u\to W_v$ and $R_{b,v,k}\colon O_b\to W_v$. The triangle of $\theta_{b,i,u,v}$ runs from $O_b$ to $V_u$ to $W_v$ and back to $O_b$, so every triangle of $X$ closes in $X^\sharp$, and identifying all $O_b$, all $V_u$ and all $W_v$ recovers $X$. Let $B=2(m-|E|)$ be the number of blocks.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:split}
$X^\sharp$ is connected, and $G^\sharp=\pi_1(X^\sharp)$ is finitely presented, torsion-free and word-hyperbolic. The quotient map $X^\sharp\to X$ induces an isomorphism of $G^\sharp$ onto a free factor of $G$, and $G\cong G^\sharp*F_{B+2r-3}$. Both $X$ and $X^\sharp$ are aspherical.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:budget}, $r|E|<Nr/4$, and $N\le m$ because the marks are distinct points of $S^h$; so $m-|E|>3N/4$ and $B\ge2$. Every $O_b$ is joined to every $V_u$ and every $W_v$, so $X^\sharp$ is connected.

Every corner of a triangle at $O$ lies in one block, every corner at $V$ joins some $L_{b,u,j}$ to some $x_{iuv}$ with the same $u$, and every corner at $W$ joins some $R_{b,v,k}$ to some $x_{iuv}$ with the same $v$. Hence the link of $X$ at $O$ is the disjoint union of the links of $X^\sharp$ at the vertices $O_b$, with the same angles, and similarly at $V$ and $W$. So every link of $X^\sharp$ is a union of connected components of a link of $X$, and \cite[Lemma~2.3]{OAI252} holds verbatim for $X^\sharp$, including the bound on link distances between $x$-directions with the same label. By \cite[Theorem~2.1]{OAI252}, $G^\sharp$ is torsion-free and word-hyperbolic, and it is finitely presented because $X^\sharp$ is finite.

Add to $X^\sharp$ a forest $T$ of $B+2r-3$ edges joining one $O_b$ to all the others, $V_0$ to every $V_u$ and $W_0$ to every $W_v$. Collapsing each tree of $T$ to a point is a homotopy equivalence from $X^\sharp\cup T$ to $X$, and since $X^\sharp$ is connected, $X^\sharp\cup T$ is homotopy equivalent to $X^\sharp$ with $B+2r-3$ circles attached at one point. This gives $G\cong G^\sharp*F_{B+2r-3}$, with $G^\sharp$ included by the map induced by $X^\sharp\to X$.

Finally, $X$ and $X^\sharp$ are finite piecewise Euclidean complexes, hence complete geodesic spaces \cite[Theorem~I.7.50]{BH1999}, and every injective loop in one of their vertex links has length at least $12\pi/5$ \cite[Lemma~2.3]{OAI252}. Their barycentric subdivisions are simplicial complexes with the same links at the original vertices, and every injective loop in the link of a new vertex has length at least $2\pi$ \cite[Section~3.1]{OAI252}. So they are nonpositively curved \cite[Theorem~II.5.2 and Lemma~II.5.6]{BH1999}, their universal covers are CAT(0) \cite[Theorem~II.4.1(2)]{BH1999}, and CAT(0) spaces are contractible \cite[Corollary~II.1.5]{BH1999}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:split-linnell}
If a group $\Lambda$ contains $G^\sharp$, then $K[\Lambda]$ has no Linnell division ring.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose paths in $X^\sharp$ from the base vertex to every vertex, and for an edge $e$ let $g_e\in G^\sharp$ be the class of the loop that runs along the path to the start of $e$, along $e$, and back along the path from its end. The triangles give $g_{x_{iuv}}=g_L^{-1}g_R$ for the sides $L,R$ of the triangle $(b,i,u,v)$, as in~\eqref{eq:triangle}, so Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} holds for the matrices $Y_i^\sharp=(g_{x_{iuv}})_{u,v\in[r]}$ over $K[G^\sharp]$, with the same proof. Let $\phi\colon K[\Lambda]\to D$ be a Linnell division ring. It suffices to show that every $\phi(Y_i^\sharp)$ is invertible, since then $\sum_i\rank_D\phi(Y_i^\sharp)=Nr$, against Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}.

Fix $i$ and write $g_{uv}=g_{x_{iuv}}$. The $x$-edges of label $i$ form a complete bipartite graph $\Theta_i^\sharp\subset X^\sharp$ between the $V_u$ and the $W_v$, with a spanning tree formed by $x_{i00}$, the $x_{iu0}$ and the $x_{i0v}$. As in Lemma~\ref{lem:free}, using the link-distance bound for $X^\sharp$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:split}, $\pi_1(\Theta_i^\sharp,W_0)$ injects into $\pi_1(X^\sharp,W_0)$. Its standard basis consists of the loops $\bar x_{iu0}x_{iuv}\bar x_{i0v}x_{i00}$ with $1\le u,v\le r-1$, so the elements $e_{uv}=g_{u0}^{-1}g_{uv}g_{0v}^{-1}g_{00}$, which are these loops moved to the base point, freely generate a subgroup $F_i$ of $G^\sharp$. Scaling by units,
\[
 \operatorname{diag}\bigl(g_{u0}^{-1}\bigr)\,Y_i^\sharp\,\operatorname{diag}\bigl(g_{0v}^{-1}g_{00}\bigr)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\\ \mathbf 1&(e_{uv})\end{pmatrix},
\]
and subtracting the first row from the others leaves $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\\0&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $M=(e_{uv}-1)_{1\le u,v\le r-1}$. So it suffices that $\phi(M)$ is invertible.

We show by induction on $k$ that $\phi(M_k)$ is invertible, with inverse entries in $D_{F_k}$, where $M_k=(e_{uv}-1)_{1\le u,v\le k}$ and $F_k=\langle e_{uv}:u,v\le k\rangle$. For $k=1$, $e_{11}$ and $1$ lie in distinct cosets of the trivial subgroup, so $(1,\Lambda)$-freeness gives $\phi(e_{11})-1\ne0$. For $k>1$ write
\[
 M_k=\begin{pmatrix}M_{k-1}&b\\c&e_{kk}-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=\bigl\langle e_{uv}:u,v\le k,\ (u,v)\ne(k,k)\bigr\rangle.
\]
The entries of $\phi(b)$, $\phi(c)$ and $\phi(M_{k-1})^{-1}$ lie in $D_C$, because $F_{k-1}\le C$. The Schur complement is $s=\phi(e_{kk})+d$ with $d=-1-\phi(c)\,\phi(M_{k-1})^{-1}\phi(b)\in D_C$. Since the $e_{uv}$ form a free basis, $e_{kk}\notin C$, so $Ce_{kk}\ne C$, and $(C,\Lambda)$-freeness gives $s\ne0$; if $d=0$, then $s=\phi(e_{kk})$ is a unit. By block elimination $\phi(M_k)$ is invertible, and its inverse has entries in $D_{F_k}$.
\end{proof}

If $G$ or $G^\sharp$ is locally indicable, then for every field $K$ of characteristic $q$ the Hughes-free division ring of $K[G]$, respectively of $K[G^\sharp]$, does not exist. For $G$ this is the (HF) part of Theorem~\ref{thm:linnell}. For $G^\sharp$ it follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:split-linnell} and a theorem of Gr\"ater \cite{Grater2020}, as stated in \cite[Theorem~2.5]{JZL2025}: the Hughes-free division ring of the group algebra of a locally indicable group, if it exists, is a Linnell division ring. Such a group would refute, in characteristic $q$, the conjecture recalled in \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025} that the Hughes-free division ring exists for every locally indicable group and every field; see \cite{JZ2021} for what is known. Whether $G$ or $G^\sharp$ is locally indicable is open.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:biorder}
Neither $G^\sharp$ nor $G$ is bi-orderable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $G^\sharp$ is a subgroup of $G$, it suffices to treat $G^\sharp$. Suppose that $\Lambda=G^\sharp$ has a total order invariant under left and right multiplication, and let $K=\F_q$. The Malcev--Neumann series $\sum_{g\in\Lambda}k_gg$ with well-ordered support form a division ring $K((\Lambda))$ containing $K[\Lambda]$ \cite{Malcev1948,Neumann1949}, and for a subgroup $C\le\Lambda$ those supported in $C$ form a division subring $K((C))$ \cite[Section~1.3]{Sanchez2011}. Let $D$ be the division closure of $K[\Lambda]$ in $K((\Lambda))$; it is epic, and $D_C\subseteq K((C))$ for every subgroup $C$. If $g_1,\dots,g_n$ lie in distinct right cosets of $C$ and $d_1,\dots,d_n\in D_C$ are nonzero, then $d_jg_j\ne0$ is supported in $Cg_j$, and these sets are disjoint, so $\sum_jd_jg_j\ne0$. Thus $D$ is a Linnell division ring for $K[\Lambda]$; this is the argument of \cite[Proposition~2.4]{JZL2025}, given there for right-invariant orders and the Dubrovin ring. This contradicts Corollary~\ref{cor:split-linnell}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:euler}
The Euler characteristics satisfy
\[
 \chi(G)=3-Nr^2-r\sum_b(s_b+t_b)+T\ >\ 3+Nr^2(3r^2-7),\qquad \chi(G^\sharp)=\chi(G)+B+2r-3.
\]
Neither $G$ nor $G^\sharp$, nor any subgroup of finite index in either, is isomorphic to a subgroup of the fundamental group of a two-complex with nonpositive immersions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:split}, $\chi(G)=\chi(X)$ and $\chi(G^\sharp)=\chi(X^\sharp)$. The complex $X$ has $3$ vertices, $Nr^2+r\sum_b(s_b+t_b)$ edges and $T$ triangles, which gives the formula; $X^\sharp$ has $B+2r$ vertices and the same edges and triangles. Since $s_b,t_b\ge r$, we have $r(s_b+t_b)\le2s_bt_b=2|I_b|$, so $r\sum_b(s_b+t_b)\le2T/r^2$ and $\chi(G)\ge3-Nr^2+(r^2-2)T/r^2$. By the degree condition of Section~\ref{sec:group}, $T/r^2=\sum_{z\notin E}|I(z)|\ge4r^2(m-|E|)>3Nr^2$, using $m-|E|>3N/4$ from the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:split}. Hence $\chi(G)>3-Nr^2+3Nr^2(r^2-2)$.

Let $G'$ be a subgroup of index $d$ in $G$ or in $G^\sharp$. It is infinite, and it is the fundamental group of a finite aspherical two-complex, a $d$-sheeted cover of $X$ or $X^\sharp$, with positive Euler characteristic $d\chi(G)$ or $d\chi(G^\sharp)$. The Euler--Poincar\'e formula for its universal cover and the vanishing of the zeroth $L^2$-Betti number of an infinite group \cite[Theorem~2.7(ix),(x)]{Lueck2003} give $b_2^{(2)}(G')-b_1^{(2)}(G')>0$. If $G'$ were isomorphic to a subgroup of the fundamental group of a two-complex with nonpositive immersions in the sense of Wise \cite{Wise2003}, it would satisfy $b_2^{(2)}(G')\le b_1^{(2)}(G')$, since it is finitely generated \cite[Proposition~2.9]{JZL2025}; the proof there follows Louder and Wilton \cite[Corollary~1.6]{LouderWilton2017}.
\end{proof}

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:questions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item Is $G$ hyperlinear? If it is, it is a hyperlinear group that is not sofic. Proposition~\ref{prop:height} excludes approximations with bounded denominators. By Proposition~\ref{prop:tracial}, a proof that $G$ is not hyperlinear cannot use only properties shared by all tracial von Neumann algebras, since $L(G)$ is one; hyperlinearity of $G$ is equivalent to an embedding of $L(G)$ in an ultrapower of the hyperfinite II$_1$ factor \cite[Theorem~8.5]{Pestov2008}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion} it would suffice, for a hyperlinear quotient that is not sofic, to find unitary approximations that keep every rectangular word away from the identity.
\item Does $\F_q[G]$ embed in a division ring? If it does, $\F_q[G]$ embeds in a division ring but has no Linnell division ring; if it does not, $G$ is a further counterexample in characteristic $q$ to \cite[Conjecture~1.1]{FSP2026}, which the zero divisors of \cite{OAI196}, \cite{Ivanov2026} and \cite{DMzd} already refute.
\item Is $G$, or $G^\sharp$, locally indicable? If so, the Hughes-free division ring of its group algebra in characteristic $q$ does not exist (Section~\ref{sec:structure}), contrary to the conjecture recalled in \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025}. Is $G$ left-orderable? If $G$ has a right-invariant order, then by \cite[Proposition~2.4]{JZL2025} and Theorem~\ref{thm:linnell} the Dubrovin ring of $K[G]$, for a field $K$ of characteristic $q$, is not a division ring, contrary to a conjecture of Dubrovin \cite{Dubrovin1987} recalled in \cite[Section~2.3]{JZL2025}.
\end{enumerate}

\item \emph{Combinatorial Gauss--Bonnet formula} \cite[(3.1)]{OAI252}. \cite[Definition~4.4 and Theorem~4.6]{McCammondWise2002}.
\item \emph{Hyperbolicity from strict angle excess} \cite[Lemma~4.1, Proposition~4.3]{OAI252}. Standard routes: \cite[Theorem~III.H.1.5]{BH1999} for the CAT(0) universal cover, or \cite[Proposition~III.H.1.2]{BH1999} for a piecewise hyperbolic metric, with \cite[Proposition~I.8.19 and Theorem~III.H.1.9]{BH1999}. \cite{OAI252} gives a direct diagram argument.
\item \emph{Stability of quasi-geodesics} \cite[Lemma~4.2]{OAI252}. \cite[Theorem~III.H.1.7]{BH1999}.
\item \emph{$\rank A\ge(\tr A)^2/\tr A^2$ for symmetric $A$} \cite[Section~2.4]{OAI252}. Classical; the proof of \cite[Lemma~2.2]{Alon2009}, and \cite[Section~3]{AHKPY2011}.
\item \emph{Real rank of a $0/1$ matrix from its degrees and four-cycles} \cite[(2.8)--(2.9) and the display after them]{OAI252}. \cite[Theorem~6]{AHKPY2011}; see Step~2 of Theorem~\ref{thm:ineq}.
\item \emph{Rank of a bounded integer matrix modulo a large prime} \cite[Lemma~2.4]{OAI252}. \cite[Lemma~1]{Soule1999}, in an argument credited there to Gabber, and \cite[Lemma~3.1]{Sauer2016}. Lemma~2.4 of \cite{OAI252} follows from Soul\'e's lemma; the two statements differ.

\item \emph{A polynomial of degree below $q-1$ sums to zero over $\F_q$} \cite[(2.13)]{OAI252}. \cite[Chapter~I, \S2.1, Lemma]{Serre1973}, whose argument \cite{OAI252} gives as the standard power-sum identity, using the root bound in place of the cyclicity of $\F_q^\times$; \cite[Theorem~3.3(i)]{Schauz2008}.
\item \emph{Block coordinates, coupled cyclic shifts and the block factorization} \cite[(2.3)--(2.5), (2.11)]{OAI252}. Not traced to an earlier source.
\item \emph{Interpolation turned into a rank certificate} \cite[(2.13)--(2.14)]{OAI252}. The ingredients are in items 9--12; the assembly was not traced to an earlier source. The same device, matrices $x_vx_v^{\mathsf T}$ of rank at most one built from low-degree interpolation that sum to zero on every full affine line, appears on the same date in \cite[Lemma~2.2 and Proposition~2.3]{OAIdf2}.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Sources proposed in discussion.} For item 13, three references were suggested. A Cartesian decomposition of a finite set \cite[Definition~2.1, Theorem~3.3]{BPS2003} and the rectangles of species theory \cite[Definitions~2.1 and~2.2]{MaiaMendez2008} give the language of a product structure on a finite set, but not the construction; \cite[Theorem~4]{Pinto2014}, on biclique covers and partitions, is not a source for this step. The labelling itself is elementary, since a set of $st$ elements is in bijection with $\Z/s\times\Z/t$. For items 12 and 14, Dvir's bound for Kakeya sets in finite fields \cite[Theorem~3]{Dvir2009} uses a related polynomial argument on lines, but contains neither the identity of item 12 nor the certificate.
